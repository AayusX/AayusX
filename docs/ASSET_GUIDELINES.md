# Asset Guidelines

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standards for all visual and media assets used throughout the AayusX portfolio.

The goal is to maintain a consistent, premium visual identity while ensuring that every asset is:
- High quality and visually appropriate.
- Authentic and accurately represented.
- Optimized for performance.
- Responsive across desktop, tablet, and mobile.
- Accessible wherever possible.
- Properly licensed and organized.
- Consistent with the approved design system.

Assets should strengthen the storytelling and engineering identity of AayusX rather than exist merely as decoration.

---

## 2. Asset Principles

Every asset must follow these principles:

1. **Quality over quantity:** Use fewer carefully selected assets rather than filling sections with unnecessary imagery.
2. **Authenticity:** Prefer real photographs, genuine project captures, official technology logos, and original visual work.
3. **Consistency:** Assets must complement the warm editorial identity of the main website.
4. **Performance:** Optimize media before including it in production.
5. **Purpose:** Every significant asset should communicate something or improve the experience.
6. **Responsiveness:** Assets must work across different screen sizes.
7. **Accessibility:** Provide meaningful alternatives for informative images.
8. **Ownership and licensing:** Only use assets that are owned, properly licensed, or permitted for use.

## 4. Personal Photography

Personal photographs are important to the identity and storytelling of the website.

### 4.1 Source and authenticity

- Prefer original photographs supplied by Aayush.
- Do not use stock photographs as substitutes for personal photographs.
- Do not generate artificial photographs that are presented as real personal experiences.
- Do not significantly alter facial identity or recognizable personal characteristics.
- Preserve natural appearance and realistic details.
- Avoid unnecessary filters and excessive retouching.

### 4.2 Image selection

Choose photographs based on:
- Relevance to the story being communicated.
- Image clarity and composition.
- Lighting and visual quality.
- Compatibility with the intended layout.
- Suitability for the cinematic introduction or About section.

Not every personal photograph belongs on the website.

### 4.3 Cropping and responsive presentation

- Use suitable crops for desktop, tablet, and mobile.
- Keep important subjects within safe cropping areas.
- Use `object-fit` and `object-position` intentionally.
- Avoid stretching or distorting photographs.
- Provide appropriate image dimensions to reduce layout shifts.

### 4.4 Privacy

- Do not publish private photographs without explicit approval.
- Do not expose unnecessary personal information through image metadata.
- Review photographs for sensitive background information before publication.

## 5. AayusX Brand Assets

The AayusX identity should remain recognizable across the website and future platforms.

### 5.1 Logo requirements

The logo must:
- Be distinctive and recognizable.
- Work at small and large sizes.
- Maintain its original proportions.
- Remain clear on light and dark backgrounds.
- Work in navigation, footer, browser icon, and social previews.
- Have a clean, professional appearance.
- Avoid unnecessary visual complexity.

### 5.2 Logo formats

Preferred formats:

| Format | Usage |
|---|---|
| SVG | Primary scalable logo |
| PNG | Transparent raster fallback |
| WebP | Optimized raster presentation where appropriate |
| ICO | Browser favicon compatibility |
| GIF | Existing animated logo, when appropriate |

Use SVG as the primary logo format whenever possible.

### 5.3 Logo variations

Maintain appropriate variations where the approved design provides them:

- Primary logo.
- Compact or symbol-only logo.
- Light-background version.
- Dark-background version.
- Monochrome version.
- Favicon or simplified small-size version.

Do not invent inconsistent logo variations.

### 5.4 Animated logo

The supplied animated logo GIF is an approved brand asset candidate.

Requirements:
- Preserve the original logo design.
- Avoid changing its proportions or identity.
- Use it only where animation adds value.
- Check visual quality and file size.
- Avoid unnecessarily repeating the animation.
- Provide a static logo fallback.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

The animated logo should not become a substitute for the primary static logo.

### 5.5 Logo reference files

The user-selected palette reference image and logo files must be treated as design references.

- Preserve the approved visual direction.
- Do not guess exact color values from memory.
- Extract and document final color values during design-system implementation.
- Confirm the correct logo assets before production use.

## 6. Technology Logos

Technology logos must be authentic, recognizable, and accurately represented.

### 6.1 Source requirements

Prefer:
1. Official brand asset repositories.
2. Official documentation and developer resources.
3. Trusted icon collections that preserve original brand artwork.

Avoid:
- AI-generated approximations.
- Unverified logo downloads.
- Generic icons presented as technology logos.
- Incorrect or outdated brand marks.

### 6.2 Visual requirements

- Preserve original proportions.
- Use authentic brand colors where permitted.
- Maintain consistent display dimensions.
- Align logos carefully.
- Avoid stretching, cropping, or recoloring official marks.
- Ensure sufficient contrast against the surrounding background.

The surrounding interface may use a consistent visual layout, but the logos themselves should retain their recognizable identities.

### 6.3 Presentation

Technology logos should support the Capabilities section rather than replace meaningful explanations.

Each relevant technology may be connected to:
- What Aayush uses it for.
- What kinds of products or systems it helps build.
- Relevant projects or experiments.

Do not imply expertise in a technology solely because its logo appears on the website.

---

## 7. Project Screenshots and Visuals

Project visuals are important evidence of actual engineering work.

### 7.1 Source requirements

Use genuine screenshots or recordings from the actual project.

- Capture the real interface.
- Use representative application states.
- Remove unnecessary browser or development-tool clutter.
- Do not fabricate product functionality.
- Do not present conceptual mockups as completed software.
- Clearly distinguish prototypes from finished projects.

### 7.2 Screenshot quality

Screenshots should:
- Be sharp and readable.
- Show meaningful interface details.
- Use consistent framing within a project presentation.
- Avoid excessive empty space.
- Preserve important interface content.
- Be captured at suitable resolutions.

### 7.3 Project galleries

Where a project contains multiple screenshots:
- Select a strong primary visual.
- Use additional images only when they provide useful information.
- Maintain consistent presentation.
- Avoid unnecessarily large galleries.
- Provide mobile-specific captures when the mobile experience matters.

### 7.4 Case studies

Important projects may include:
- Hero screenshot.
- Product interface details.
- Architecture or workflow diagrams.
- Relevant technical visuals.
- Short video demonstrations.

Only include visuals that genuinely support the case study.

---

## 8. Editorial and Supporting Images

Images used in About, Journey, Experiments, and Writing should reinforce the content.

Guidelines:
- Prefer original or properly licensed imagery.
- Use illustrations when they communicate a concept more clearly.
- Maintain the warm editorial visual identity.
- Avoid generic AI-generated decorative images.
- Avoid unrelated stock photography.
- Keep image composition consistent with the surrounding layout.

Supporting imagery should never compete with the actual content.

---

## 9. Video Assets

Videos may be used for:
- Project demonstrations.
- Selected portfolio previews.
- Cinematic storytelling.
- Technical experiments.

### 9.1 Video requirements

- Use short, purposeful clips.
- Prefer MP4 with H.264 for broad compatibility.
- Consider WebM where it provides meaningful performance benefits.
- Compress videos appropriately.
- Avoid unnecessary high-resolution footage.
- Provide a poster image.
- Provide fallback content when video cannot play.

### 9.2 Playback behaviour

- Avoid autoplay with sound.
- Use muted playback only where appropriate.
- Do not force users to watch a video.
- Pause or stop unnecessary playback when content is no longer visible.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Provide controls for meaningful user-facing video content.

### 9.3 Mobile considerations

- Use smaller or simpler video alternatives where appropriate.
- Avoid loading large video files immediately.
- Ensure video content does not interfere with navigation or scrolling.
- Keep important information available without requiring video playback.

---

## 10. 3D Assets

Three-dimensional content is an optional enhancement, not a requirement for every section.

The portfolio may use Three.js and React Three Fiber for selected interactive experiences.

### 10.1 Model requirements

3D assets should:
- Have a clear purpose.
- Match the website's visual identity.
- Use clean geometry and suitable materials.
- Avoid unnecessary polygon complexity.
- Have optimized textures.
- Be tested on mobile and lower-powered devices.

### 10.2 Preferred formats

| Format | Usage |
|---|---|
| GLB | Preferred packaged 3D model format |
| GLTF | Flexible 3D scene and model format |
| KTX2 | Compressed textures where supported |
| WebP | Supporting raster textures where appropriate |

Use GLB where a self-contained model is convenient.

### 10.3 Performance requirements

- Lazy-load nonessential 3D scenes.
- Avoid loading large models during the initial page render.
- Reduce model complexity where possible.
- Compress textures and geometry.
- Limit unnecessary lights and shadows.
- Avoid excessive post-processing.
- Provide a static fallback.
- Adapt scene complexity to device capability.

### 10.4 Interaction requirements

- Motion should be intentional.
- Avoid continuous animation without a clear purpose.
- Prefer user-controlled interactions.
- Support touch input where relevant.
- Avoid making essential content accessible only through 3D interaction.

If a 3D scene negatively affects usability or performance, simplify or remove it.

---

## 11. Icons and Interface Graphics

Interface icons should be visually consistent.

Requirements:
- Use a consistent icon library or carefully maintained custom SVGs.
- Prefer clean vector artwork.
- Maintain consistent stroke weight and sizing.
- Use recognizable symbols.
- Include accessible labels for icon-only controls.
- Avoid decorative emoji as substitutes for interface icons.

Do not mix unrelated icon styles throughout the website.

## 12. Asset Optimization

Performance is a primary product requirement.

### 12.1 Image formats

| Format | Recommended usage |
|---|---|
| AVIF | High-efficiency photographic images where supported |
| WebP | General optimized image delivery |
| JPEG | Compatibility fallback for photographs |
| PNG | Transparency or cases requiring lossless raster images |
| SVG | Logos, icons, and vector illustrations |

Use responsive image delivery where appropriate.

### 12.2 Image optimization

- Resize images to their actual display requirements.
- Avoid uploading unnecessarily large originals.
- Generate responsive variants for important images.
- Compress images while preserving visual quality.
- Specify image width and height or aspect ratio.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
- Prioritize the actual above-the-fold hero image when appropriate.

Do not lazy-load the primary image if doing so delays the main visual experience.

### 12.3 Font assets

- Load only required font families and weights.
- Prefer WOFF2 for web font delivery.
- Subset fonts where practical, ensuring Nepali glyph coverage is preserved.
- Avoid loading unused font styles.
- Use appropriate fallback fonts.
- Apply font-display strategies to prevent invisible text.

### 12.4 Media loading

- Avoid loading all media during initial page load.
- Use lazy loading for nonessential assets.
- Use preloading only for genuinely critical resources.
- Prevent layout shifts with reserved dimensions.
- Avoid unnecessary duplicate downloads.

## 13. Asset Naming Convention

Use descriptive, lowercase, hyphen-separated filenames.

Recommended pattern:

`category-description-variant.extension`

Examples:

```text
brand-aayusx-primary.svg
brand-aayusx-symbol.svg
brand-aayusx-animated.gif

profile-aayush-main.webp
profile-aayush-about.webp

project-sanjog-dashboard.webp
project-statlite-map.webp

technology-react.svg
technology-typescript.svg

experiment-3d-scene.glb
intro-memory-01.webp
```

Guidelines:
- Use meaningful names.
- Avoid spaces.
- Avoid random generated filenames.
- Include variants only when useful.
- Keep filenames consistent.
- Do not overwrite original source assets without preserving a backup when needed.

## 15. Accessibility and Alternative Text

Informative images must have meaningful alternative text.

Examples:

```tsx
<img
  src="/assets/profile/profile-aayush-main.webp"
  alt="Portrait of Aayush Bhandari"
/>
```

Decorative images should use empty alternative text where appropriate:

```tsx
<img
  src="/assets/experiments/decorative-pattern.svg"
  alt=""
/>
```

Additional requirements:
- Avoid overly long alt descriptions.
- Do not repeat surrounding text unnecessarily.
- Describe the meaningful content of diagrams.
- Provide captions or explanations for complex technical visuals.
- Ensure controls containing images have accessible names.

---

## 16. Licensing and Attribution

Every externally sourced asset must have a known usage basis.

Before adding an asset:
- Check its license.
- Confirm whether commercial use is permitted.
- Check attribution requirements.
- Preserve required notices.
- Record the original source.
- Avoid copyrighted assets without permission.

Suggested internal asset record:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Filename | Asset filename |
| Source | Original source or creator |
| License | Applicable license |
| Attribution | Required credit, if any |
| Usage | Where the asset is used |
| Status | Approved, pending, or rejected |

Do not assume that an asset is free to use simply because it is publicly accessible.

---

## 17. Asset Loading and Runtime Behaviour

Assets should not compromise the experience.

- Avoid unnecessary loading animations.
- Provide sensible fallbacks for unavailable assets.
- Handle failed image and video requests gracefully.
- Avoid broken image placeholders.
- Prevent oversized media from blocking interaction.
- Keep loading behaviour predictable.
- Test asset paths in production builds.
- Ensure case-sensitive filenames work correctly in deployment environments.

---

## 18. Responsive Asset Strategy

Different screen sizes may require different assets or presentation strategies.

### Desktop
- Higher-resolution project visuals where useful.
- Full cinematic composition.
- Richer 3D experiences when device capability allows.

### Tablet
- Balanced image composition.
- Reduced animation complexity where necessary.
- Comfortable media sizing.

### Mobile
- Mobile-appropriate image crops.
- Smaller media variants.
- Simplified 3D and animation.
- Touch-friendly interactions.
- No unnecessary large downloads.

Responsive presentation should preserve the intended story, not merely shrink desktop content.

---

## 19. Asset Review Checklist

Before an asset enters production, verify:

### Quality
- [ ] Is it visually clear?
- [ ] Is it relevant to the content?
- [ ] Does it fit the design direction?
- [ ] Is it authentic and accurately represented?

### Technical
- [ ] Is the format appropriate?
- [ ] Is the file optimized?
- [ ] Are dimensions suitable?
- [ ] Does it load correctly?
- [ ] Does it work in production?

### Accessibility
- [ ] Is meaningful alt text provided?
- [ ] Is the asset usable on mobile?
- [ ] Does it respect reduced-motion preferences where relevant?

### Legal and privacy
- [ ] Is the asset owned or properly licensed?
- [ ] Is attribution recorded where required?
- [ ] Has private or sensitive information been removed?

### Performance
- [ ] Is it loaded at the appropriate time?
- [ ] Is lazy loading used where appropriate?
- [ ] Does it introduce layout shifts?
- [ ] Does it negatively affect Lighthouse results?

---

## 20. Final Asset Principles

The AayusX portfolio should use visual assets to communicate identity, demonstrate real engineering work, and create a memorable experience.

The final implementation must prioritize:

1. Authentic personal and project imagery.
2. Consistent AayusX brand assets.
3. Official and recognizable technology logos.
4. High-quality project presentation.
5. Purposeful animation and 3D.
6. Optimized media delivery.
7. Responsive and accessible presentation.
8. Proper licensing and privacy.

**The objective is not to use the largest number of impressive assets. It is to use the right assets, at the right time, with the right quality.**
