

# AayusX Content Guidelines

**File:** `docs/CONTENT_GUIDELINES.md`  
**Project:** AayusX Personal Portfolio  
**Status:** Draft  
**Brand:** AayusX  
**Content Direction:** Personal, Technical, Editorial, Authentic

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how content should be written, organized, presented, and maintained across the AayusX website.

The goal is to communicate the person behind AayusX, demonstrate genuine technical ability, present real work, and express entrepreneurial ambition without exaggeration.

Every piece of content should contribute to a clear understanding of:

- Who is behind AayusX.
- What they are interested in.
- What they build.
- How they approach problems.
- What they are learning.
- What they want to create in the future.

The website should feel like a genuine person documenting their work and development—not a company marketing page or an AI-generated portfolio.

---

## 2. Brand Voice

### 2.1 Core Voice

AayusX uses a balanced voice combining:

- Professional credibility.
- Personal expression.
- Technical understanding.
- Curiosity.
- Entrepreneurial ambition.

The writing should feel confident without being arrogant, ambitious without being unrealistic, and technical without becoming difficult to understand.

### 2.2 Writing Characteristics

| Characteristic | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Clear | Communicate ideas directly |
| Natural | Sound human, not automated |
| Confident | Explain work without unnecessary hesitation |
| Honest | Never exaggerate experience or results |
| Technical | Demonstrate genuine understanding |
| Personal | Include authentic thoughts and experiences |
| Ambitious | Communicate direction without making false claims |
| Concise | Remove unnecessary words |

### 2.3 Avoid

Do not use:

- Generic AI marketing language.
- Empty motivational statements.
- Unverified claims.
- Inflated professional titles.
- Excessive buzzwords.
- Repetitive introductions.
- Artificially dramatic storytelling.
- Unnecessary self-promotion.
- Fake statistics or achievements.

---

## 3. Writing Style

### 3.1 Sentence Structure

Prefer clear, direct sentences.

Use shorter paragraphs for readability.

Explain complex technical ideas in understandable language without removing important technical details.

### 3.2 Headlines

Headlines should be memorable and meaningful.

They may be expressive, but they must communicate something relevant.

Avoid vague headlines that could belong to any developer portfolio.

### 3.3 Paragraphs

- Keep paragraphs focused on one idea.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Use meaningful transitions.
- Break long technical explanations into sections.
- Use lists when they improve comprehension.

### 3.4 First-Person Writing

Use first-person language where appropriate.

Examples:

- "I build software to solve practical problems."
- "I'm exploring ways to make technology more useful."
- "This project taught me a different approach to..."
- "I started experimenting with..."

Do not force first-person language into every sentence.

AayusX represents a technology builder interested in turning ideas and real-world problems into useful digital solutions.

**The technology entrepreneur who builds useful solutions.**

### 4.2 Personal Introduction

The website should establish the relationship between the brand and its creator.

Use:

- AayusX as the primary brand identity.
- Aayush Bhandari as the person behind the brand.

### 4.3 Professional Identity

The content may communicate interests and work involving:

- Software development.
- Full-stack development.
- Artificial intelligence.
- Engineering.
- Product development.
- Digital solutions.
- Entrepreneurship.

Only describe technologies, skills, and responsibilities that are genuinely supported by the user's work.

## 5. Homepage Content

### 5.1 Objective

The homepage should establish a memorable first impression and encourage visitors to explore further.

It should quickly communicate:

1. Who is behind AayusX.
2. What they build.
3. What makes their approach interesting.
4. Where visitors can explore their work.

### 5.2 Content Structure

Recommended content:

- Primary headline.
- Short personal introduction.
- Supporting statement.
- Primary navigation or exploration action.
- Selected project highlights.
- Brief capabilities overview.

### 5.3 Headline Rules

The main headline should be:

- Distinctive.
- Personal.
- Easy to understand.
- Connected to technology and building.
- Short enough to work visually.

Avoid generic phrases such as:

- "Welcome to my digital world."
- "Passionate developer creating amazing experiences."
- "Turning dreams into reality."
- "Innovating the future of technology."

These phrases do not communicate a distinctive identity.

## 6. About Section

### 6.1 Objective

The About section should introduce the actual person behind AayusX.

It should communicate personality, background, interests, and direction.

### 6.2 Recommended Content

- Personal introduction.
- Educational background.
- Interest in technology.
- Development journey.
- Approach to building solutions.
- Current ambitions.
- An invitation to join the community, collaborate, or work with Yugya.

- Use accurate educational information.
- Do not invent employment history.
- Do not exaggerate years of experience.

## 7. Journey Section

### 7.1 Objective

The Journey section documents meaningful development milestones.

It should feel like a collection of important moments rather than a conventional employment résumé.

### 7.2 Suitable Milestones

- Important projects.
- Technical discoveries.
- Learning breakthroughs.
- Hackathons.
- Collaborations.
- Community involvement.
- Leadership responsibilities.
- Meaningful achievements.

### 7.3 Milestone Structure

Each milestone should contain:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Title | Short, descriptive name |
| Date | Verified date or period |
| Category | Project, learning, event, leadership, etc. |
| Description | What happened |
| Significance | Why it mattered |
| Media | Relevant image or visual, if available |

### 7.4 Writing Rules

Explain the significance of each milestone.

Do not simply list technologies or activities.

Avoid inventing dates, awards, rankings, or outcomes.

## 8. Projects Section

### 8.1 Objective

Projects are the strongest evidence of technical ability.

The content should explain what was built, why it matters, and what the user contributed.

### 8.2 Project Selection

Only completed and sufficiently polished projects belong in the main Projects gallery.

Incomplete work should be presented separately in Experiments or another clearly labeled development area.

### 8.3 Project Description Structure

Every project should have:

1. Project name.
2. Short introduction.
3. Problem or motivation.
4. Main functionality.
5. User's actual role.
6. Technologies used.
7. Current project status.
8. Relevant links.

### 8.4 Project Summary

The short summary should explain the product in one or two sentences.

It should communicate value rather than merely list technologies.

**Weak example:**

"React, Node.js, MongoDB, Tailwind CSS and JavaScript project."

**Better example:**

"A web application designed to simplify the way users organize and manage their daily work."

The better example still requires the project to genuinely provide that functionality.

### 8.5 Project Case Studies

Important projects may use dedicated case-study pages.

Recommended structure:

1. Overview.
2. Problem.
3. Goals.
4. Research or planning.
5. Solution.
6. Architecture and technology.
7. Implementation.
8. Challenges.
9. Decisions and trade-offs.
10. Results.
11. Lessons learned.
12. Links and demonstrations.

Only include sections that have meaningful information.

### 8.6 Results and Metrics

Use measurable results only when they are verified.

Never invent:

- User counts.
- Revenue.
- Performance improvements.
- Download numbers.
- Business impact.
- Adoption statistics.

If reliable measurements are unavailable, explain the functionality and implementation instead.

### 8.7 Project Status

Use clear status labels where necessary:

- Completed.
- In development.
- Experimental.
- Archived.

Do not imply that a private or unavailable demonstration is publicly accessible.

Experiments document technical curiosity and creative exploration.

### 9.2 Suitable Content

- UI experiments.
- Interactive visualizations.
- Animation studies.
- Technical prototypes.
- Algorithms.
- Performance experiments.
- Unusual interface concepts.
- Research and learning explorations.

### 9.3 Experiment Structure

Each entry may contain:

- Title.
- Short description.
- Motivation.
- Technical approach.
- What was explored.
- Current status.
- Lessons or discoveries.
- Demonstration where available.

Do not make unfinished prototypes appear like completed products.

## 10. Capabilities Section

### 10.1 Objective

The Capabilities section should explain what the user can build.

It must communicate practical ability rather than becoming a decorative technology logo collection.

### 10.2 Content Structure

Organize capabilities into meaningful categories, such as:

- Software development.
- Web development.
- Application development.
- Backend systems.
- AI and intelligent applications.
- Engineering and problem-solving.

Only include categories supported by actual experience or ongoing learning.

### 10.3 Technology Presentation

For each relevant technology:

- Display its authentic logo.
- Use its correct brand colors.
- Provide its name.
- Explain its practical purpose where useful.
- Connect it to relevant projects when possible.

### 10.4 Skill Representation

Do not use fake percentages or unsupported proficiency ratings.

Demonstrate skills through:

- Completed projects.
- Technical explanations.
- Code and implementation decisions.
- Experiments.
- Real-world functionality.

## 11. Writing Section

### 11.1 Objective

The Writing section provides space for technical knowledge, personal insights, and documented learning.

It should demonstrate how the user thinks, explores problems, and communicates ideas.

### 11.2 Content Categories

**Technical writing**
- Tutorials.
- Engineering explanations.
- Development workflows.
- Technical discoveries.
- Architecture decisions.
- Problem-solving approaches.

**Personal writing**
- Learning experiences.
- Reflections.
- Development challenges.
- Lessons from projects.
- Entrepreneurial observations.
- Personal growth.

### 11.3 Article Structure

Recommended structure:

1. Title.
2. Short introduction.
3. Main content.
4. Relevant examples.
5. Supporting visuals or code.
6. Key observations.
7. Conclusion.
8. Related articles where appropriate.

### 11.4 Technical Accuracy

- Verify technical claims.
- Use correct terminology.
- Distinguish personal opinion from established facts.
- Keep examples functional.
- Explain assumptions.
- Reference external sources where appropriate.

### 11.5 Writing Tone

Articles should sound like a knowledgeable learner and builder sharing genuine understanding.

Do not pretend to be an authority on subjects that have not been sufficiently explored.

## 12. Contact Section

### 12.1 Objective

Make professional enquiries simple and approachable.

### 12.2 Messaging

The Contact section should communicate openness to relevant opportunities.

Possible subjects:

- Software projects.
- Product collaboration.
- Technical discussions.
- Startup ideas.
- Professional enquiries.

### 12.3 Form Content

Fields:

- Name.
- Email.
- Subject.
- Message.

Keep labels clear and instructions concise.

Do not ask visitors for unnecessary personal information.

### 12.4 Response Feedback

Use clear messages for:

- Submission in progress.
- Successful delivery.
- Submission failure.
- Validation errors.

Never claim an enquiry was delivered unless the backend confirms successful processing.

## 13. Personal Storytelling

### 13.1 Objective

The website should make the person behind the work memorable.

Storytelling should connect experiences, interests, decisions, and ambitions.

### 13.2 Storytelling Principles

- Use real experiences.
- Include meaningful details.
- Explain why decisions mattered.
- Show learning and development.
- Connect past experiences to current interests.
- Avoid artificial drama.

### 13.3 Cinematic Introduction

The memory introduction should use genuine personal photographs and meaningful visual storytelling.

The writing and imagery should communicate ambition, curiosity, personal growth, and the direction the user is working toward.

Do not use generic motivational quotes or invented personal history.

## 14. Language and Localization

The website supports:

- English.
- Nepali.

### Requirements

- Translate all important interface text.
- Translate navigation labels.
- Translate buttons and messages.
- Translate section introductions.
- Support localized metadata.
- Preserve the meaning and tone across languages.
- Ensure Nepali content is natural and readable.

### Translation Rules

- Do not rely on literal machine translation without review.
- Preserve intent rather than translating word-for-word.
- Use consistent technical terminology.
- Keep product and technology names recognizable.
- Avoid layouts that break with longer translations.

English is the default content language.

Nepali should feel like a fully supported language, not an afterthought.

---

## 15. Search and Metadata Content

Every important page should have appropriate metadata.

### Required Elements

- Page title.
- Meta description.
- Canonical URL.
- Open Graph title.
- Open Graph description.
- Social preview image where appropriate.
- Language information.

### Metadata Rules

- Make titles descriptive.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Write unique descriptions.
- Do not use misleading claims.
- Ensure metadata matches visible page content.
- Provide appropriate English and Nepali metadata.

---

## 16. Social Presence

Use only genuine, relevant social profiles.

Primary profiles:

- GitHub.
- LinkedIn.
- Professional email.

Additional profiles may be included when they are active and relevant.

### Rules

- Do not link to inactive or placeholder accounts.
- Do not invent follower counts.
- Do not display fake social proof.
- Use recognizable platform identities.
- Provide accessible labels.

---

## 17. Collaboration and Contact Content

The site is not a job application. It should make it easy for the right people to start a conversation about building something together.

### Requirements

- Provide a contact route from the About section.
- Invite visitors to join the community, collaborate on a project, or work with Yugya.
- Describe the kinds of work and problems that fit, without inventing clients or outcomes.
- Keep contact details accurate.
- Maintain consistency between the contact page and the rest of the site.

## 18. Content Data Structure

Content should be organized in a maintainable format.

Suggested project structure:

```text
src/
  content/
    en/
      home
      about
      journey
      projects
      experiments
      capabilities
      writing
      contact

    ne/
      home
      about
      journey
      projects
      experiments
      capabilities
      writing
      contact

    projects/
    experiments/
    articles/
    case-studies/
```

This is a conceptual structure. Adapt it to the existing application and chosen i18n/content architecture.

### Content Principles

- Keep content separate from presentation.
- Avoid duplicated content.
- Make updates straightforward.
- Use structured project data.
- Keep translations organized.
- Avoid hardcoding large content blocks into UI components.

## 19. Content Quality Checklist

Before publishing any content:

### Accuracy

- [ ] Are all claims true?
- [ ] Are dates verified?
- [ ] Are project details accurate?
- [ ] Are technologies represented honestly?
- [ ] Are outcomes supported by evidence?

### Writing

- [ ] Is the message clear?
- [ ] Does it sound natural?
- [ ] Is it concise?
- [ ] Does it avoid generic AI language?
- [ ] Does it communicate something meaningful?

### Presentation

- [ ] Does it fit the visual hierarchy?
- [ ] Are headings descriptive?
- [ ] Are paragraphs readable?
- [ ] Are images relevant?
- [ ] Are links functional?

### Localization

- [ ] Is the English version correct?
- [ ] Is the Nepali version natural?
- [ ] Are technical terms consistent?
- [ ] Does the layout work in both languages?

---

## 20. Final Content Principle

AayusX content should show the real person behind the technology.

It should demonstrate what has been built, explain how problems are approached, document what is being learned, and communicate where the journey is heading.

**Be ambitious in vision, precise in claims, honest about progress, and clear in communication.**
